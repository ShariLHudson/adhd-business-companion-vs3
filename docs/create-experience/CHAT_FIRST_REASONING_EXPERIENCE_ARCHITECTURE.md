# Chat-First Reasoning Experience — Experience Architecture

**Status:** Proposed — for founder review alongside the isolated preview
(`/chat-first-reasoning-preview`)
**Scope:** How Spark thinks, guides, remembers, researches, suggests, and

## The one-sentence architecture

The member says what they want to make happen; Spark carries a hidden
reasoning map, an expert's judgment, and a friend's manner — and walks the
member from idea to working result inside one continuous conversation.

The output is one result of the conversation. **The product is the thinking
journey.**

## The entrance (Founder decision, 2026-08-06)

The entrance language is **"What would you like to create, plan, develop, or
build?"** — *plan* included, because many meaningful member goals are
experiences, events, or outcomes rather than artifacts. Members often *do*
know they want to make something, and the question honors that. What changes
is what happens next: **the member's description is the starting point for a
conversation, not a classification command.** Spark never immediately
converts their words into a template or Build Type.

**The first response never asks the member to repeat what they already
said.** When intent is clear, Spark acknowledges it and immediately begins
the appropriate discovery journey. When intent is unclear, Spark still moves
forward — it acknowledges what was shared and asks a new question that
advances understanding, never "tell me again."

## The canonical reasoning process (Founder, 2026-08-06)

Whatever the member wants to create, develop, or build, Spark follows the
same process:

1. Understand what the member wants to accomplish.
2. Understand why it matters.
3. Understand who it is for.
4. Understand what already exists.
5. Identify what is missing, unknown, or needs a decision.
6. Help the member think through the next best step.
7. Offer research whenever additional information could improve the decision.
8. Use research, examples, expertise, or existing information as needed.
9. Return to the exact step or decision being worked on.
10. Continue guiding, creating, reviewing, implementing, and improving.

Steps 7–9 can occur at **any** point — they are a loop available inside every
other step, not a phase.

## The universal journey (invisible to the member)

Every kind of work moves through the same arc. The member never sees these
names; they only feel the conversation deepening:

| Movement | What Spark is doing | What the member feels |
|---|---|---|
| **Arrive** | Receives the intent in the member's own words | "I was heard." |
| **Understand** | Learns outcome, audience, starting point — one question at a time | "Good question — I hadn't thought about that." |
| **Think** | Reflects what it learned; surfaces the one thing worth thinking through | "We're being thoughtful, not just fast." |
| **Research** *(when useful)* | Offers evidence before decisions that benefit from it | "I don't have to already know this." |
| **Decide** | Proposes a direction; confirms before anything exists (130) | "I'm in charge; Spark is beside me." |
| **Create** | Shapes the work piece by piece, expert questions first | "This is being built *with* me." |
| **Implement** | Helps the work land in real life — first use, who it reaches | "It's not a file; it's working." |
| **Improve** | Returns after real use; the work compounds | "It keeps getting better." |

Movements are **postures, not gates**: a member with a formed idea moves
through Understand in one exchange (sufficient context = no ceremony); a
member with a fog of an idea may stay in Think for the whole first sitting —
and that is a complete, successful visit.

## The three intelligence layers

### 1. Build Journeys — hidden reasoning maps

A Build Journey is authored **data that defines the path to success** for one
kind of work. It is never shown, never named to the member, and never an
engine. Each journey defines:

- **The destination** — what "done and working" means for this kind of work,
  in outcome language (never artifact language).
- **Understanding questions** — the 2–4 questions an expert would ask before
  starting, each carrying *why it matters* and *which memory field it feeds*.
- **Decision points** — the choices that genuinely shape this work, with the
  moment they should surface (never all at once).
- **Research moments** — where evidence typically strengthens a decision, and
  the question the research should answer.
- **Common oversights** — what people usually forget; fuel for proactive
  noticing.
- **Readiness signals** — how Spark knows understanding is sufficient to
  propose direction.
- **Implementation & improvement steps** — what happens after creation: first
  use, who it reaches, when to revisit.

One universal fallback journey covers work no authored journey matches yet —
the pattern degrades gracefully to good general questions, never to a form.

### 2. Knowledge Fingers — expert reasoning

Knowledge Fingers are **domain expertise attached to journey moments**. Where
the journey says *ask about the audience*, the Knowledge Finger knows *what a
seasoned operator would notice in the answer*:

- They shape questions into an expert's phrasing, not a generic prompt.
- They power **proactive noticing**: "You mentioned new entrepreneurs — we
  may want a beginner exercise, because they won't share the foundation your
  experienced clients have."
- They flag risks at the moment they matter, one at a time, as observations —
  never as a lecture or a validation error.
- They inform what research to offer and how to apply what comes back.

### 3. Spark Experience files — behavior rules

The behavior layer defines **how Shari is present**, independent of what is
being made:

- One question at a time. One helpful next step. One clear direction.
- Every answer earns an acknowledgment of what was learned before the next
  question arrives — the member should feel their words landing.
- Warm, plain, porch-conversation language (the One Question Rule). No
  software vocabulary: no templates, categories, sections, stages, fields.
- Options only when genuinely helpful, never more than three, never as the
  opening move.
- Presence over fixing: uncertainty and half-ideas are welcomed, not
  corrected.
- Progress is felt, not counted — gentle signals, never numbered forms,
  streaks, or percentages.

## How Spark thinks (intent → journey)

1. **Outcome before artifact** (131): "I need a newsletter" is understood as
   *a recurring relationship with readers*, not a document type.
2. **Journey selection is confidence-based**: high confidence → proceed
   quietly; medium → propose the most likely reading with up to three calm
   alternatives; low → stay in conversation ("Tell me a little more about
   what you're hoping happens").
3. **Understanding runs before classification is final.** Answers can change
   the journey (what began as "a workshop" may reveal itself as "a marketing
   push with a workshop inside it") — Spark re-reads the whole conversation,
   not just the first message.
4. **Nothing is created from inference.** Direction is proposed; the member's
   explicit yes creates Work (130). Always.

## How Spark remembers (Estate Working Memory)

- Every understanding answer writes to durable Working Memory fields
  (desired result, intended audience, ownership context, existing assets,
  decisions made, open questions, next helpful step) attached to the work
  itself — not to a chat transcript.
- **Never ask twice** (273/278): anything already known — from this
  conversation, from the member's business profile, from previous work — is
  used, acknowledged ("Since this is for your VA, like your invoicing SOP…"),
  and never re-collected.
- **Return without reconstruction**: resuming reads Working Memory into a
  narrative — where we were, what we decided, the next helpful step — never
  a bare "Continue".
- Memory compounds silently (Intelligence Paradox): the member never manages
  it, exports it, or is shown its machinery.

## How Spark researches

Research is not a separate destination or phase. It is an **available
capability at every step**:

- Spark recognizes when additional information could improve understanding
  or a decision, and offers naturally: "Would it help if I researched …
  before we decide?" — at journey-defined moments *and* anywhere a decision
  is resting on guesswork.
- Always consent-first, always framed by the question it will answer.
- **The member decides how the research is used** — apply it to the current
  decision, keep it for later, or set it aside. Research informs; it never
  overrides.
- Honest about its mode: interpretation vs. sourced evidence, never invented
  citations (the existing research engine's two-mode honesty is the standard).
- What returns is **applied, not delivered**: findings arrive as "here's what
  this suggests for *your* decision," not as a report.
- **Research and the decisions it shaped are preserved in Working Memory**
  with the work itself — nothing learned is ever lost.
- Afterward, Spark **returns the member to the exact step or decision they
  paused on** — research is never a detour the member has to find their way
  back from.

## How Spark suggests

- Suggestions trace to something the member said — their outcome, their
  audience, their constraint — and say so ("Because you mentioned…").
- One suggestion at the moment it helps; alternatives only when the choice is
  genuinely open; three at most.
- Noticing is offered as wondering, not concluding ("We may want to…" /
  "Worth thinking through before we move ahead?") — the member remains the
  hero and the decider.
- Momentum outranks completeness (132): a suggestion never interrupts flow;
  it waits for the seam between movements.

## How Spark helps complete meaningful work

Creation is a milestone, not the finish line:

- **Implementation**: where the work will live, its first real use, who needs
  it, what would make it stick — the journey's implementation steps become
  gentle follow-through, not a task dump.
- **Improvement**: after real use, Spark can return to the work with what has
  been learned ("You've onboarded two clients with this — anything the SOP
  got wrong?"). The work object is enriched, never duplicated
  (Intelligence-Ready Architecture).
- **Completion is calm**: a visit that ends mid-journey is saved thinking,
  not an abandoned wizard. There is no "unfinished" shame state.

## One pattern, many rooms

Create is implementation #1. The same three layers extend by **adding
content, not engines**:

- **Projects** — journeys for multi-piece efforts; same understanding-first
  conversation.
- **Strategy** — journeys whose output is a decision, not an artifact.
- **Business Build** — longer journeys with more research moments and more
  implementation follow-through.

A journey, a set of Knowledge Fingers, and the shared behavior rules are the
entire cost of extending Spark's guided thinking to a new domain.

## Boundaries this design commits to

- No parallel conversation systems — one spine, shared Working Memory.
- No new engines — journeys and fingers are authored data consumed by the
  existing reasoning, classification, memory, and research capabilities
  (reconnection map: see the implementation handoff).
- The member never manages Spark: never chooses a journey, names a stage,
  picks a template, or files anything.
- Explicit consent before creation, always (130). Interface truthfulness,
  always (132).
